## 2.6 Inadequacy of Local Axioms {#2.6}

A critical realization confronts us when we examine the behavior of extended causal chains because we find that local rules alone fail to prevent global paradoxes from emerging in the transitive closure of the graph. Our primitives successfully police individual links yet remain blind to longer paths that bend around to touch their own origins to create time machines out of mediated influence. We must address the subtle danger that a sequence of individually valid steps could collectively form a structure that violates the logical consistency of the whole and creates a conflict between local legality and global causality. This forces us to confront the limits of reductionism in a system where global topology emerges from local rules.

The system remains vulnerable to transitive snarls where an event indirectly becomes its own ancestor through a sequence of valid steps if we rely solely on local constraints to govern the evolution. This failure destroys the partial order of the universe and collapses the distinction between past and future to render the timeline incoherent and physically impossible. A universe that permits such circular dependencies cannot support computation or evolution because the state of the system would become undefined and riddled with logical contradictions that prevent the consistent propagation of information. We cannot allow the local freedom of the graph to destroy its global consistency.

We address this inadequacy by exposing the specific failure modes of local axioms such as the reflexive loop in a 3-cycle or the symmetric dependency in a bowtie configuration to diagnose the root of the instability. This diagnosis demonstrates the necessity of a third global constraint to enforce acyclicity across all scales and ensures that the arrow of time remains consistent not just for immediate neighbors but for the entire history of the universe. This moves our theory from a description of local interactions to a framework for global consistency and ensures that the causal order is an invariant property.

### 2.6.2 Lemma: Effective Influence {#2.6.2}

:::info[**Effective Influence via Strictly Increasing Simple Witnesses of Mediated Influence**]
:::

Let $uRv$ mean that there is a directed path from $u$ to $v$ of at least two edges, with pairwise distinct vertices and strictly increasing active heights. The relation $R$ is irreflexive and every witness admits a split at an interior vertex into two strictly increasing subpaths.

### 2.6.2.1 Proof: Effective Influence {#2.6.2.1}

:::tip[**Effective Influence via Decomposition of a Simple Increasing Witness**]
:::

**I. Witness and Endpoint Conditions**

For $P=(x_0,\ldots,x_k)$ with $k\ge2$, put $h_i=H(x_{i-1},x_i)$ for $1\le i\le k$ (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />). The witness conditions are

$$
\forall\,0\le i<j\le k,\ x_i\ne x_j,\qquad
\forall\,1\le i<k,\ h_i<h_{i+1}.
$$

A diagonal pair would give a witness with $0<k$ and $x_0=x_k=u$. At the distinct indices $0,k$, simplicity requires $x_0\ne x_k$. Thus

$$
(u,u)\in R\Longrightarrow (x_0=x_k\land x_0\ne x_k),\qquad
R\cap\{(u,u):u\in V\}=\varnothing .
$$

**II. Restriction to Two Subpaths**

For $1\le j<k$, set $P_-=(x_0,\ldots,x_j)$ and $P_+=(x_j,\ldots,x_k)$. Every edge of either path is an edge of $P$. Their height lists are $(h_1,\ldots,h_j)$ and $(h_{j+1},\ldots,h_k)$, respectively. For consecutive heights inside either list the comparisons are inherited from the displayed witness conditions, and at the shared vertex $x_j$ the comparison is $h_j<h_{j+1}$.

**III. Concatenation under a Strict Join**

Conversely, let $P_-,P_+$ be simple increasing paths of positive length with intersection of vertex sets exactly $\{x_j\}$ and with terminal and initial heights satisfying $h_j<h_{j+1}$. The concatenated vertex sequence uses the join once. A repeated vertex would have to repeat within one subpath, or belong to both outside the join, and each possibility contradicts a hypothesis. All height comparisons are

$$
h_1<\cdots<h_j,\qquad h_j<h_{j+1},\qquad
h_{j+1}<\cdots<h_k .
$$

These give one increasing list of length at least two, so the concatenation witnesses $x_0Rx_k$ (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />).

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.2.2 Commentary: Causal Mediation and Simultaneity Evasion {#2.6.2.2}

:::info[**Single Witnesses and Transitive Closure**]
:::

The two descriptions of mediated influence use different composition operations. A single witness is a spatial path with pairwise distinct vertices and increasing heights. Its split into two subpaths preserves the comparisons because both pieces come from the same witness. Either piece can be a direct edge if the split lies one step from an endpoint.

Taking the transitive closure composes pairs of the mediated relation. The relation composition only identifies the common endpoint. It can combine witnesses whose heights reset at that endpoint, or whose vertex lists would repeat a position when concatenated. This is how a diagonal of the closure can arise even though every individual witness has distinct endpoints and the relation itself has no diagonal. The closure is built from nonempty chains of mediated pairs.

### 2.6.3 Lemma: Strict Timestamps {#2.6.3}

:::info[**Strict Timestamps via Strict and Weak Height Filtering**]
:::

Let $R_{\le}$ use the same simple paths of length at least two as $R$, with nondecreasing heights in place of strictly increasing heights. Then $R\subseteq R_{\le}$, and the inclusion can be strict even when both relations have acyclic closure.

### 2.6.3.1 Proof: Strict Timestamps {#2.6.3.1}

:::tip[**Strict Timestamps via Comparison of Strict and Weak Inequalities**]
:::

**I. Domain and Objects**

Both tests use the same underlying spatial path family (**Strict Timestamps** <Ref id="2.6.3" label="§2.6.3" />) (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />).

**II. Inclusion**

For every witness of $uRv$, each comparison $h_i<h_{i+1}$ implies $h_i\le h_{i+1}$, giving $uR_{\le}v$.

**III. Equal-Height Witness**

The graph $a\xrightarrow{0}b\xrightarrow{0}c$ has outgoing sets $\{b\},\{c\},\varnothing$ in that order. Its only two-edge path is $(a,b,c)$ and $c$ has no successor. The height pair $(0,0)$ fails $0<0$ and satisfies $0\le0$. Hence

$$
R=\varnothing,\qquad R_{\le}=\{(a,c)\}.
$$

No pair in $R_{\le}$ starts at $c$, so $R_{\le}^2=\varnothing$ and $R_{\le}^+=\{(a,c)\}$. Also $R^+=\varnothing$. Both closures have empty diagonal. Acyclicity of the weak relation therefore permits equal heights along a mediated path.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.3.2 Commentary: Timestamp Strictness {#2.6.3.2}

:::info[**Active Heights and Occurrence Order**]
:::

Replacing a strict comparison with a weak one introduces spatial paths along edges of equal height. This change matters even on an outward two-path, where no directed cycle is possible. The example isolates the effect of the height predicate because the graph and the underlying family of simple paths are fixed.

The occurrence order uses the supporting inputs of a creation event, whereas the spatial witness test compares the active heights along a path in one snapshot. Strict increase for occurrence dependencies comes from the source maximum at creation. The absence of a cycle in a relation constructed with weak comparisons supplies no numerical inequality for the heights of its individual edges. An increasing numerical sequence is a sufficient witness condition under the stated path test.

### 2.6.4 Lemma: Failure of Reflexivity {#2.6.4}

:::info[**Violation of Irreflexivity through the Geometric Quantum**]
:::

Let $R$ denote mediated simple-path influence on the timestamped directed four-cycle $A\xrightarrow1B\xrightarrow2C\xrightarrow3D\xrightarrow4A$. Then $AR^+A$ in the nonempty transitive closure, although the simple-path relation $R$ is itself irreflexive (**Axiom 3 Acyclic Effective Causality** <Ref id="2.7.1" label="§2.7.1" />).

### 2.6.4.1 Proof: Failure of Reflexivity {#2.6.4.1}

:::tip[**Demonstration of Self-Influence via Transitive Analysis**]
:::

**I. The Two Simple Witnesses**

The vertices $A,B,C,D$ are distinct. The sequences $(A,B,C)$ and $(C,D,A)$ have no repeated vertices, have length two, and satisfy the respective height tests $1<2$ and $3<4$ (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />). Hence

$$
A\,R\,C,\qquad C\,R\,A.
$$

**II. Nonempty Transitive Closure**

A witness for $xR^+y$ is a finite sequence $x=x_0R x_1R\cdots R x_m=y$ with $m\ge1$. The sequence of intermediate vertices $(A,C,A)$ has two $R$-steps, so

$$
A\,R\,C\land C\,R\,A\Longrightarrow AR^+A.
$$

The composed witness is an $R$-chain. It is not required to be one simple spatial path.

**III. Irreflexivity of the Original Filter**

If $x\,R\,x$, a simple path of length at least two would have identical first and last vertices, contrary to pairwise vertex distinctness. Therefore $\neg(x\,R\,x)$ for every $x$, even in this countermodel.

**IV. Conclusion**

$$
\boxed{\forall x,\ \neg(x\,R\,x),\qquad AR^+A.}
$$

The closure violates the required causal irreflexivity (**Failure of Reflexivity** <Ref id="2.6.4" label="§2.6.4" />).

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.4.2 Commentary: Non-Reflexive Causality {#2.6.4.2}
:::tip[**Elimination of Self-Causation at the Micro-Scale**]
:::

The simple-path definition already excludes a self-pair in the original mediated influence relation. The independent causal requirement concerns cycles formed by chains of those influence steps. A chain can return to its starting vertex even though every individual witness has distinct vertices and increasing internal heights. The four-vertex example isolates that distinction: it supplies two valid mediated witnesses, while their composition belongs to a transitive closure rather than to the original class of simple chronological paths.

This separation also explains why a closed spatial walk is insufficient as a simple-path witness. Repeating the starting vertex changes the mathematical object being used. The temporal filter and the nonempty transitive closure must therefore remain visible in both statements and proofs. Acyclicity of the closure gives an actual strict order; its preservation by a particular bounded scheduler check is a further dynamical obligation. The countermodel motivates that obligation without claiming that a chronology-violating spatial assignment is an admissible physical trajectory.

### 2.6.5 Lemma: Failure of Asymmetry {#2.6.5}

:::info[**Mutual Influence from Increasing Spatial Arcs**]
:::

Let $G$ be a directed cycle of length $L\ge4$. There is a height assignment under which two vertices $a,b$ satisfy $aRb$ and $bRa$ along simple spatial arcs with disjoint interiors.

### 2.6.5.1 Proof: Failure of Asymmetry {#2.6.5.1}

:::tip[**Failure of Asymmetry via Separate Height Assignments on the Two Cycle Arcs**]
:::

**I. Domain and Objects**

The arc endpoints define mediated pairs when each arc passes the strict test (**Failure of Asymmetry** <Ref id="2.6.5" label="§2.6.5" />) (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />).

**II. Splitting the Perimeter**

Index the perimeter $x_0,\ldots,x_{L-1},x_0$. Put $a=x_0$ and $b=x_2$. The two directed arcs have lengths $2$ and $L-2$, and their interiors are $\{x_1\}$ and $\{x_3,\ldots,x_{L-1}\}$.

**III. Witness Heights**

Assign heights $1,2$ on the first arc and $1,2,\ldots,L-2$ on the second. Each arc has at least two edges, pairwise distinct vertices, and strictly increasing heights. Thus

$$
a\mathrel R b,\qquad b\mathrel R a,\qquad (a,a)\in R^+ .
$$

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.5.2 Commentary: Asymmetry Constraints {#2.6.5.2}

:::tip[**Arc Endpoints and Mediated Closure**]
:::

The perimeter splits at two chosen vertices into arcs that share their endpoints and have disjoint interiors. Each witness is a simple path on its own. The heights along the second arc can begin again at a low value, because its comparison test is independent of the first arc's terminal height.

The two opposite mediated arrows produce a diagonal after taking transitive closure. The joined spatial traversal repeats its starting vertex, so it cannot be a simple witness for a diagonal of the original relation. Keeping these two operations separate explains how local checks of individual increasing paths can coexist with a failure of acyclicity in their full relational closure. The construction assigns heights to the perimeter separately on the two arcs with no shared edges.

### 2.6.6 Lemma: Causal Acyclicity vs. Spatial Triangulation {#2.6.6}

:::info[**Independence of Spatial Area Closures from Causal Timeline Ordering**]
:::

Let $G_{\mathrm{space}} = (V, E)$ denote the instantaneous Spatial State Graph, and let $G_{\mathrm{event}} = (E, \prec)$ denote the Causal History Poset. Then the existence of directed 3-cycles representing discrete spatial area elements in $G_{\mathrm{space}}$ does not induce or construct directed causal cycles in $G_{\mathrm{event}}$, which remains a strict directed acyclic graph.

### 2.6.6.1 Proof: Causal Acyclicity vs. Spatial Triangulation {#2.6.6.1}

:::tip[**Topological Distinctions between Spatial Boundaries via Chronological Ordering**]
:::

**I. Two Distinct Incidence Relations**

Let $G_{space}=(V,E)$ be the spatial graph, and let $\mathcal E$ be the set of edge-creation events. The event graph has event vertices and causal dependency edges:

$$
G_{event}=(\mathcal E,D),\qquad D\subseteq\prec,
$$

where $\prec$ is the stipulated strict history order (**Causal Acyclicity vs. Spatial Triangulation** <Ref id="2.6.6" label="§2.6.6" />). Spatial adjacency is a relation on $V$; causal precedence is a relation on $\mathcal E$. They are not identified by sharing an incidence symbol (**Causal Graph Substrate** <Ref id="1.4.1" label="§1.4.1" />).

**II. No Cycle in a Strict Event Order**

Suppose the event graph contained a positive-length directed cycle

$$
\varepsilon_0\ D\ \varepsilon_1\ D\ \cdots\ D\ \varepsilon_{m-1}\ D\ \varepsilon_0.
$$

Since $D\subseteq\prec$, transitivity of $\prec$ gives, inductively,

$$
\varepsilon_0\prec\varepsilon_j\quad(1\le j<m),\qquad
\varepsilon_0\prec\varepsilon_{m-1}\prec\varepsilon_0
\ \Longrightarrow\ \varepsilon_0\prec\varepsilon_0.
$$

This contradicts irreflexivity. For $m=1$, the first edge already gives that contradiction. Therefore $G_{event}$ has no directed cycles.

**III. A Spatial Triangle Does Not Supply the Missing Dependency**

The spatial witness is a triangle with distinct supports

$$
e_1=(u,v),\qquad e_2=(v,w),\qquad e_3=(w,u)
$$

(**Geometric Quantum** <Ref id="2.3.3" label="§2.3.3" />). Denote their creation events by $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3$. A consistent event order can have

$$
\varepsilon_1\prec\varepsilon_2\prec\varepsilon_3,
\qquad H(\varepsilon_1)=1,\ H(\varepsilon_2)=2,\ H(\varepsilon_3)=3.
$$

The spatial equality of the final endpoint of $e_3$ and the first endpoint of $e_1$ is an equality in $V$. It does not assert the event dependency $\varepsilon_3\prec\varepsilon_1$. In the concrete order the missing dependency would require $H(\varepsilon_3)<H(\varepsilon_1)$, or $3<1$. In the abstract order,

$$
\varepsilon_1\prec\varepsilon_2\prec\varepsilon_3
\Longrightarrow \varepsilon_1\prec\varepsilon_3,\qquad
(\varepsilon_1\prec\varepsilon_3)\land(\varepsilon_3\prec\varepsilon_1)
\Longrightarrow\varepsilon_1\prec\varepsilon_1 .
$$

Both are contradictions. Thus

$$
\boxed{C_3\subseteq G_{space}\ \text{is compatible with}\
\operatorname{Cycles}(G_{event})=\varnothing}
$$

under the stated separate strict event-order structure.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.6.2 Commentary: Causal and Spatial Interactions {#2.6.6.2}

:::info[**Prevention of Closed Timelike Loops in Curvature Fields**]
:::

The structural decoupling between spatial triangulation and causal history resolves a fundamental tension in discrete quantum gravity. In Quantum Braid Dynamics, space is constructed from oriented **3-cycles** that tile the graph into simplicial 2-complexes, endowing the substrate with metric area and discrete curvature. However, because edge creation events are assigned discrete, monotonically advancing timestamps $H(e) \in \mathbb{N}_0$, spatial closed loops remain purely relational boundaries that do not circulate causal influence through historical time.

At the cosmological origin ($t=0$), the pre-geometric Bethe tree represents an instantaneous spatial leaf where all constituent edges share the ground-state timestamp $H=0$ (**Topological Tunneling** <Ref id="3.4.2" label="§3.4.2" />). This timestamp degeneracy ensures that tree 2-paths are not strictly monotone under historical accumulation ($0 \not< 0$). Consequently, the Acyclic Pre-Check `pre_check_aec` permits the deterministic first-tick parallel burst to nucleate spatial 3-cycles across unperturbed branches (**Ignition Probability** <Ref id="3.4.5" label="§3.4.5" />) without creating causal closed loops.

If spatial loops were permitted to circulate influence across monotonically increasing historical timestamps, the causal substrate would degenerate into closed timelike curves, destroying the well-founded partial order of history. Enforcing strict chronological monotonicity on causal paths ensures that the emergent spacetime satisfies discrete global hyperbolicity. While local constructibility rules (Axioms 1 and 2) govern the formation of spatial simplices, the global transitivity of **Acyclic Effective Causality** <Ref id="2.7.1" label="§2.7.1" /> protects the timeline against large-scale acausal reconvergence, securing a consistent Lorentzian background for matter and gauge interactions.

The separation result consumes a distinct strict event-order structure as specified in the statement. It does not derive that structure merely from assigning birth numbers to the edges of a spatial graph. A concrete constructor must identify its event objects and dependency edges, and establish that updates preserve their order. Once that structure is supplied, a spatial triangle is not a closed causal history simply because its spatial endpoints close.

### 2.6.7 Lemma: Least Constructive Countermodel {#2.6.7}

:::info[**Existence of a Minimum Four-Vertex Constructive Countermodel via Two Return Chords**]
:::

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the class of finite timestamped graphs satisfying Axiom 1 and the constructive geometric predicate. The least vertex count in $\mathcal C$ with $(u,u)\in R^+$ for some vertex $u$ is four.

### 2.6.7.1 Proof: Least Constructive Countermodel {#2.6.7.1}

:::tip[**Minimality via Triangle Acyclicity and Two Return-Chord Insertions**]
:::

**I. Reduction to a Directed Triangle**

Let $G$ denote a graph satisfying **Axiom 1 Directed Causal Link** <Ref id="2.1.1" label="§2.1.1" /> on at most three vertices. If $uR^+u$, then a finite nonempty chain has the form

$$
u=u_0Ru_1R\cdots Ru_m=u,\qquad m\ge1.
$$

For each $1\le j\le m$, let $P_j=(x_{j,0},\ldots,x_{j,k_j})$ denote a witness of $u_{j-1}Ru_j$ (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />). Its endpoints satisfy

$$
k_j\ge2,\qquad x_{j,0}=u_{j-1},\qquad x_{j,k_j}=u_j,
\qquad x_{j,k_j}=x_{j+1,0}\quad (j<m).
$$

Let $s_j$ denote $\sum_{\ell=1}^j k_\ell$, with $s_0=0$. Define $W=(w_0,\ldots,w_{s_m})$ as the concatenation with $w_{s_{j-1}+a}=x_{j,a}$ for $0\le a\le k_j$. The endpoint equality makes the two assignments at $s_j$ agree. For $0\le a<k_j$,

$$
(w_{s_{j-1}+a},w_{s_{j-1}+a+1})=(x_{j,a},x_{j,a+1})\in E,\qquad
w_0=x_{1,0}=u=w_{s_m},\qquad s_m\ge2m>0.
$$

Let $(i,j)$ denote equal-vertex indices in $W$ with $0\le i<j\le s_m$ minimizing $d=j-i$. Any equal pair $w_a=w_b$ with $i\le a<b<j$ would give a smaller positive difference $b-a\le j-1-i<d$. Thus $w_i,\ldots,w_{j-1}$ are distinct, so $d\le |V|\le3$. For the two shorter possibilities,

$$
\begin{aligned}
d=1&\ \Longrightarrow\ (w_i,w_{i+1})=(w_i,w_i)\in E,\\
d=2&\ \Longrightarrow\ w_{i+1}\ne w_i,\quad
       (w_i,w_{i+1})\in E,\quad (w_{i+1},w_{i+2})=(w_{i+1},w_i)\in E .
\end{aligned}
$$

The first contradicts irreflexivity and the second contradicts the reciprocal-edge exclusion. Hence $d=3$. Let $(x,y,z)$ denote $(w_i,w_{i+1},w_{i+2})$. Since these are all three vertices of $G$,

$$
\{(x,y),(y,z),(z,x)\}\subseteq E,\qquad
E\setminus\{(x,y),(y,z),(z,x)\}
 \subseteq\{(x,x),(y,y),(z,z),(y,x),(z,y),(x,z)\}.
$$

All six pairs in the second set are forbidden by Axiom 1. Thus $E=\{(x,y),(y,z),(z,x)\}$.

**II. Mediated Closure on a Triangle**

Let $x\to y\to z\to x$ denote the triangle, with respective heights $a,b,c$. A simple witness in this graph has at most two edges, and membership in $R$ requires at least two. Each start vertex has one outgoing edge and its successor also has one, so the complete list of candidates is

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{witness} & \text{ordered pair in }R & \text{height test}\\ \hline
x\to y\to z & (x,z) & a<b\\
y\to z\to x & (y,x) & b<c\\
z\to x\to y & (z,y) & c<a
\end{array}
$$

The three tests together imply $a<b<c<a$, hence $a<a$. At least one test fails. In each row below, $S$ denotes the relation formed by the other two candidate pairs. The only composition within $S$ joins its two arrows at their common vertex:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{failed test} & S & S^2\\ \hline
a<b & \{(y,x),(z,y)\} & \{(z,x)\}\\
b<c & \{(x,z),(z,y)\} & \{(x,y)\}\\
c<a & \{(x,z),(y,x)\} & \{(y,z)\}
\end{array}
$$

The terminal vertex of the sole pair in $S^2$ has no outgoing pair in $S$ in each row. Thus $S^n=\varnothing$ for $n\ge3$. The inclusion $R\subseteq S$ yields $R^+\subseteq S\cup S^2$, whose displayed pairs have distinct endpoints. This contradicts $uR^+u$.

**III. Construction of a Four-Vertex Accretion**

Let $G_0$ denote the graph on $V=\{A,B,C,D\}$ with edges $C\xrightarrow{1}A$, $A\xrightarrow{2}B$, and $B\xrightarrow{4}D$. Let $E_i$ and $O_i(v)$ denote the edge set and outgoing neighbors of $v$ after $i$ insertions. The base satisfies

$$
E_0=\{(C,A),(A,B),(B,D)\},\qquad
E_0^{-1}=\{(A,C),(B,A),(D,B)\},\qquad E_0\cap E_0^{-1}=\varnothing.
$$

Define $r$ as the rank function with $(r(C),r(A),r(B),r(D))=(0,1,2,3)$. Every base edge increases $r$ by one. A directed cycle $v_0,\ldots,v_\ell=v_0$ in $G_0$ would yield

$$
0=r(v_\ell)-r(v_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\bigl(r(v_{j+1})-r(v_j)\bigr)=\ell>0.
$$

Let $I_0(v)$ denote the incoming neighbors of $v$ in $G_0$. Pairing each incoming tail with each outgoing head lists the two-paths:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
v & I_0(v) & O_0(v) & I_0(v)\times O_0(v) \\ \hline
A & \{C\} & \{B\} & \{(C,B)\}\\
B & \{A\} & \{D\} & \{(A,D)\}\\
C & \varnothing & \{A\} & \varnothing\\
D & \{B\} & \varnothing & \varnothing
\end{array}
$$

The two endpoint pairs differ, and $\{(C,B),(A,D)\}\cap E_0=\varnothing$. Each of the two paths is therefore unique among paths of length at most two with its endpoints, as required of the base (**Independence of Axioms 1 and 2** <Ref id="2.5.1" label="§2.5.1" />).

For the first candidate $B\to C$, the support from $C$ to $B$ is obtained from the $A$ row of the table:

$$
\Pi_{\ell\le2}^{\,G_0}(C,B)=\varnothing\cup\{(C,A,B)\}=\{(C,A,B)\},
\qquad
\{(B,C),(C,B)\}\cap E_0=\varnothing.
$$

The first equality establishes candidate-relative PUC and the second excludes an existing edge or its reverse (**Principle of Unique Causality (PUC)** <Ref id="2.3.4" label="§2.3.4" />). The $B$ row and the timestamp recurrence yield

$$
E_1=E_0\cup\{(B,C)\},\qquad
H_1(B,C)=1+\max\bigl(\{H_0(A,B)\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)=1+\max\{2,0\}=3
$$

(**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />).

For the second candidate $D\to A$, the outgoing sets yield the two-path family

$$
\{(A,v,w):v\in O_1(A),\ w\in O_1(v)\}
=\{(A,B,C),(A,B,D)\}.
$$

Here $O_1(A)=\{B\}$ and $O_1(B)=\{C,D\}$. The subfamily ending at $D$ consists of $(A,B,D)$ alone, and $D\notin O_1(A)$ excludes the direct bypass. Hence

$$
\Pi_{\ell\le2}^{\,G_1}(A,D)=\varnothing\cup\{(A,B,D)\}=\{(A,B,D)\},
\qquad
\{(D,A),(A,D)\}\cap E_1=\varnothing.
$$

These conditions establish PUC and preserve Axiom 1. Since the only incoming edge of $D$ is $B\to D$ and its height is unchanged, the recurrence yields

$$
E_2=E_1\cup\{(D,A)\},\qquad
H_2(D,A)=1+\max\bigl(\{H_1(B,D)\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)=1+\max\{4,0\}=5.
$$

The restriction of either $E_1$ or $E_2$ to $V\setminus\{A\}$ is $\{(B,C),(B,D)\}$. Both heads have no outgoing edge in this restriction, so every directed cycle contains $A$. For a simple cycle $(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell=v_0)$ with $v_j=A$, the sequence $(v_j,\ldots,v_{\ell-1},v_0,\ldots,v_j)$ is a rotation starting and ending at $A$. Since $O_i(A)=\{B\}$ and $O_i(B)=\{C,D\}$ for $i=1,2$, the two possible two-edge prefixes are $(A,B,C)$ and $(A,B,D)$. Their continuations use

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
& O_i(C) & O_i(D) \\ \hline
i=1 & \{A\} & \varnothing\\
i=2 & \{A\} & \{A\}
\end{array}
$$

The table allows only a return to $A$ or termination after the two-edge prefix. Continuation past a return would repeat $A$, contrary to simplicity. Therefore the cycle families, with each cycle written from $A$, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{(A,B,v,A):v\in O_1(B),\ A\in O_1(v)\}
  &=\{(A,B,C,A)\},\\
\{(A,B,v,A):v\in O_2(B),\ A\in O_2(v)\}
  &=\{(A,B,C,A),(A,B,D,A)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both insertions satisfy the cycle condition of the constructive predicate.

**IV. Attainment of a Diagonal**

The final adjacency and height lists yield the following distinct-vertex witnesses with increasing heights:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{witness in }G_2 & \text{height comparison} & \text{relation}\\ \hline
A\to B\to C & 2<3 & AR C\\
C\to A\to B & 1<2 & CR B\\
B\to D\to A & 4<5 & BR A
\end{array}
$$

These three relations compose in the nonempty closure:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(A,C),(C,B)\in R &\ \Longrightarrow\ (A,B)\in R^2,\\
(A,B)\in R^2,\ (B,A)\in R &\ \Longrightarrow\ (A,A)\in R^3\subseteq R^+.
\end{aligned}
$$

The four-vertex construction and the exclusion for at most three vertices establish the minimum.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.7.2 Commentary: Candidate and Snapshot Uniqueness {#2.6.7.2}

:::info[**Difference between Proposal Support and Final-Graph Path Multiplicity**]
:::

In the four-vertex example the support of the second proposal starts at $A$, whose only successor is $B$. The resulting snapshot contains the separate paths $B\to C\to A$ and $B\to D\to A$. They share endpoints and have different intermediates, so the static diagnostic fails at $(B,A)$. The proposal checked the path from $A$ to $D$ in the input snapshot. The static diagnostic checks every pair of endpoints in the output snapshot.

The example starts from a timestamped base, as permitted in the constructive predicate. Its argument recomputes the heights of the two new edges from the corresponding input snapshots. Those computations fix the heights needed for the three mediated pairs in the final graph. The base heights are specified data for an independence witness. Establishing the same witness from a particular initialized evolution requires proving that such a base is reachable under its update rules.

### 2.6.8 Lemma: Least Static Countermodel {#2.6.8}

:::info[**Existence of a Minimum Five-Vertex Static Countermodel via the Butterfly**]
:::

Let $\mathcal S$ denote the class of finite timestamped graphs satisfying Axiom 1 and the static geometric diagnostic. The least vertex count in $\mathcal S$ with $(u,u)\in R^+$ for some vertex $u$ is five.

### 2.6.8.1 Proof: Least Static Countermodel {#2.6.8.1}

:::tip[**Minimality via Shared-Edge Triangles and the Five-Vertex Butterfly**]
:::

**I. Exclusion of Two Triangles on Four Vertices**

Let $G$ denote a graph on at most four vertices satisfying Axiom 1 and the static geometric diagnostic (**Independence of Axioms 1 and 2** <Ref id="2.5.1" label="§2.5.1" />). Let $S,T$ denote the vertex sets of two directed triangles. Then

$$
|S\cap T|=|S|+|T|-|S\cup T|\ge3+3-4=2.
$$

If $|S\cap T|=3$, then $S=T$. Let $E_S$ and $E_T$ denote the triangle edge sets. For any unordered pair $\{p,q\}$ in $S$, both intersections of the triangle edge sets with $\{(p,q),(q,p)\}$ are singletons. If the singletons differed, their union would include both orientations in $E$, contrary to Axiom 1. Thus

$$
E_S\cap\{(p,q),(q,p)\}=E_T\cap\{(p,q),(q,p)\}
\qquad(\{p,q\}\subseteq S,\ p\ne q).
$$

The union over the three unordered pairs yields $E_S=E_T$.

For distinct triangles, $|S\cap T|=2$. Let $\{u,v\}$ denote the intersection, with the shared edge oriented $u\to v$, and let $x$ and $y$ denote the third vertices in $S$ and $T$, respectively. Closure of each triangle determines its other two edges:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{vertex set} & \text{edge set} & \text{two-path from }v\text{ to }u \\ \hline
\{u,v,x\} & \{(u,v),(v,x),(x,u)\} & (v,x,u)\\
\{u,v,y\} & \{(u,v),(v,y),(y,u)\} & (v,y,u)
\end{array}
$$

Since $x\ne y$, the set of simple paths of length at most two from $v$ to $u$ includes both distinct triples and has cardinality at least two. This contradicts uniqueness under the static diagnostic. Thus $G$ contains at most one directed triangle.

**II. Localization of a Closed Mediated Chain**

Suppose $uR^+u$. Then $u=u_0Ru_1R\cdots Ru_m=u$ for some $m\ge1$, and each link admits a simple witness (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />). Let $W=(w_0,\ldots,w_q)$ denote the closed walk given by substituting those witnesses at the shared endpoints (**Least Constructive Countermodel** <Ref id="2.6.7" label="§2.6.7" />). For $0\le i<q$, let $x$ and $y$ denote $w_i$ and $w_{i+1}$, respectively. The return walk from $y$ to $x$ along the other edges of $W$ is

$$
(w_{i+1},\ldots,w_{q-1},w_0,\ldots,w_i),
$$

where the first segment is empty if $i+1=q$, because $w_q=w_0$. Let $P=(p_0,\ldots,p_k)$ denote a shortest walk from $p_0=y$ to $p_k=x$ using edges of $W$. If $p_a=p_b$ for $0\le a<b\le k$, deleting that segment yields

$$
P'=
\begin{cases}
(p_0,\ldots,p_a,p_{b+1},\ldots,p_k), & b<k,\\
(p_0,\ldots,p_a), & b=k .
\end{cases}
$$

In the first case $(p_a,p_{b+1})=(p_b,p_{b+1})$ is an edge of $W$. In the second case $p_a=p_k=x$, so the terminal vertex is preserved. Both walks have $k-(b-a)<k$ edges, contrary to the choice of $P$. Thus the vertices of $P$ are distinct. Since $x\ne y$, prefixing $x$ to $P$ yields the simple cycle $(x,p_0,\ldots,p_k=x)$ containing $x\to y$.

The static diagnostic requires each of these cycles to have length three. Since $W$ has an edge, there is at least one such triangle, and Part I establishes that there is at most one. Let $T$ denote this triangle, let $E_T$ denote its edges, and let $R_T$ denote mediated influence computed using only these edges and their original heights. For the vertices $u_0,\ldots,u_m$ of the original $R$-chain, each witness uses only edges of $W$, and $E(W)\subseteq E_T$. Consequently,

$$
(u_{j-1},u_j)\in R_T\quad(1\le j\le m),\qquad u_0=u_m=u
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (u,u)\in R_T^+.
$$

The closure on the triangle is irreflexive for any assignment of its active heights (**Least Constructive Countermodel** <Ref id="2.6.7" label="§2.6.7" />). Thus $\{(v,v):v\in V(T)\}\cap R_T^+=\varnothing$, contrary to $(u,u)\in R_T^+$.

**III. Verification of the Five-Vertex Static Diagnostic**

Let $G$ denote the graph on $\{A,B,C,D,Z\}$ given by

$$
A\xrightarrow{1}Z,\quad Z\xrightarrow{2}D,\quad D\xrightarrow{3}C,\quad
C\xrightarrow{4}Z,\quad Z\xrightarrow{5}B,\quad B\xrightarrow{6}A.
$$

The edge set and its reverse satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{(A,Z),(Z,D),(D,C),(C,Z),(Z,B),(B,A)\},\\
E^{-1}&=\{(Z,A),(D,Z),(C,D),(Z,C),(B,Z),(A,B)\},\\
E\cap E^{-1}&=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

Each pair in $E$ has distinct coordinates, establishing Axiom 1 together with the disjointness. The restriction to $V\setminus\{Z\}$ is $\{(B,A),(D,C)\}$, whose heads have no outgoing edges in that restriction. Thus every simple cycle contains $Z$. Following each first edge out of $Z$ yields the following successive choices:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{first edge} & \text{next edge} & \text{first return edge} & \text{cycle}\\ \hline
Z\to B & B\to A & A\to Z & (Z,B,A,Z)\\
Z\to D & D\to C & C\to Z & (Z,D,C,Z)
\end{array}
$$

The only outgoing edges from $Z$ are the two first edges in the table, and each subsequent choice is unique until the return. Continuing past that return would repeat $Z$. Hence the listed triangles are the only simple cycles up to rotation.

Every two-path through a middle vertex $w$ comes from pairing an incoming edge of $w$ with an outgoing edge of $w$. The complete choices and their endpoint pairs are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|l|l}
w & \text{incoming tails} & \text{outgoing heads}
  & \text{paths} & \text{endpoint pairs}\\ \hline
A & B & Z & B\to A\to Z & (B,Z)\\
B & Z & A & Z\to B\to A & (Z,A)\\
C & D & Z & D\to C\to Z & (D,Z)\\
D & Z & C & Z\to D\to C & (Z,C)\\
Z & A,C & B,D & A\to Z\to B,\ A\to Z\to D,\ C\to Z\to B,\ C\to Z\to D
  & (A,B),\ (A,D),\ (C,B),\ (C,D)
\end{array}
$$

Each triple in the table has distinct vertices. Let $D_2(s)$ denote the set of destinations of its two-paths starting at $s$, and let $O(s)$ denote the destinations of the direct edges from $s$. The row-by-row counts and intersections are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
s & D_2(s) & \text{two-path count from }s & O(s) & D_2(s)\cap O(s)\\ \hline
A & \{B,D\} & 2 & \{Z\}   & \varnothing\\
B & \{Z\}   & 1 & \{A\}   & \varnothing\\
C & \{B,D\} & 2 & \{Z\}   & \varnothing\\
D & \{Z\}   & 1 & \{C\}   & \varnothing\\
Z & \{A,C\} & 2 & \{B,D\} & \varnothing
\end{array}
$$

In each row $|D_2(s)|$ equals the number of two-paths in that row. Thus each destination occurs once among the two-paths from $s$. The empty intersections exclude a direct edge with the same endpoints. Together with the two triangle cycles, this establishes the static diagnostic.

**IV. Attainment of a Diagonal**

The graph contains the two simple directed witnesses

$$
A\xrightarrow{1}Z\xrightarrow{2}D,\qquad
D\xrightarrow{3}C\xrightarrow{4}Z\xrightarrow{5}B\xrightarrow{6}A.
$$

The first satisfies $1<2$, and the second satisfies $3<4<5<6$. The two paths yield $AR D$ and $DR A$, so

$$
(A,D),(D,A)\in R\ \Longrightarrow\ (A,A)\in R^2\subseteq R^+.
$$

The five-vertex example and the exclusion for graphs on at most four vertices establish the minimum.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.8.2 Commentary: Butterfly Accretion {#2.6.8.2}

:::info[**Butterfly Construction via Two Return-Chord Insertions**]
:::

The butterfly can also be built from the acyclic base $\{AZ,ZD,DC,ZB\}$ with respective heights $1,2,3,5$. Insert $CZ$ across $ZDC$ and then $BA$ across $AZB$. Each supporting path is already present in its input and remains unique among paths of length at most two with the same endpoints in the final graph. Since the construction only inserts edges, a competing route in either input would also occur in the final graph. Each candidate edge is absent in its own input.

Before each insertion, the unique incoming edge at the source has height $3$ or $5$, giving the new heights $4$ and $6$. Each intermediate snapshot is a subgraph of the final butterfly, whose simple cycles are triangles. The final graph therefore meets both geometric tests. In the four-vertex construction the triangles share an edge, creating two return routes across that edge in the final snapshot. In the butterfly the triangles share a vertex, and the enumerated two-paths retain distinct endpoint pairs.

### 2.6.9 Proof: Inadequacy of Local Axioms {#2.6.9}

:::tip[**Inadequacy of Local Axioms via Composition of Local Influence Witnesses**]
:::

**I. Spatial Witnesses**

Every $R$ witness is a simple increasing spatial path (**Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" />). Weak height filtering allows additional equal-height witnesses (**Strict Timestamps** <Ref id="2.6.3" label="§2.6.3" />).

**II. Closure Failure**

A composite cycle gives a diagonal pair in $R^+$ (**Failure of Reflexivity** <Ref id="2.6.4" label="§2.6.4" />). In particular, the two increasing arcs of the directed perimeter yield $(a,b),(b,a)\in R$, and hence $(a,a)\in R^+$ (**Failure of Asymmetry** <Ref id="2.6.5" label="§2.6.5" />).

The four-vertex accretion satisfies Axiom 1 and the constructive geometric predicate with a diagonal in $R^+$ (**Least Constructive Countermodel** <Ref id="2.6.7" label="§2.6.7" />). The five-vertex butterfly satisfies Axiom 1 and the static geometric diagnostic with a diagonal in $R^+$ (**Least Static Countermodel** <Ref id="2.6.8" label="§2.6.8" />).

**III. Spatial Closure**

Spatial triangle closure can coexist with acyclic mediated closure (**Causal Acyclicity vs. Spatial Triangulation** <Ref id="2.6.6" label="§2.6.6" />). The two local constraints thus permit both acyclic and cyclic mediated closures.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.9.1 Corollary: Global Constraint {#2.6.9.1}

:::info[**Necessity of an Explicit Global Constraint via Causal Unidirectionality**]
:::

A physical theory requires a well-defined causal ordering (a "direction of time"). The proven failure of Axioms 1 and 2 to entail such an order necessitates a third axiom. This axiom must explicitly forbid states containing causal paradoxes, acting as a global topological constraint.

Q.E.D.

### 2.6.Z Implications and Synthesis {#2.6.Z}

:::note[**Inadequacy of Local Axioms**]
:::

Taken together, **Inadequacy of Local Axioms** <Ref id="2.6.1" label="§2.6.1" /> and the mediated path hierarchy demonstrate precisely why local timestamp filtering fails to produce a strict partial order under Axioms 1 and 2 alone. Even when every single link satisfies directedness and every witness in **Effective Influence** <Ref id="2.6.2" label="§2.6.2" /> passes **Strict Timestamps** <Ref id="2.6.3" label="§2.6.3" /> (tightening weak inequality $R_\le$ to strict pathwise increase $R$), monotonicity applies only inside individual simple paths rather than as a global potential on vertices. Concatenating separate locally valid paths whose edge heights reset at a shared vertex creates both reflexive pairs $(u, u) \in R^+$ (**Failure of Reflexivity** <Ref id="2.6.4" label="§2.6.4" />) and mutual reachability $(a, b), (b, a) \in R$ across long cycles or two-triangle butterflies, breaking strict partial ordering of $R^+$ while every individual edge and $2$-path neighborhood remains locally valid.

Combining that negative closure result with **Causal Acyclicity vs. Spatial Triangulation** <Ref id="2.6.6" label="§2.6.6" /> isolates a dual structural lesson separating topology from chronology across the two-graph substrate. Neither direction of reduction works: purging spatial cycles of length $L \ge 4$ never guarantees acyclic transitive mediation in $R^+$ (since two Axiom-2-compliant $3$-cycles joined at a vertex already form a cyclic witness, **Independence of Axiom 3** <Ref id="2.7.6" label="§2.7.6" />), whereas closing spatial triangles in $G_{\mathrm{space}}$ never by itself forces causal paradoxes once an independent chronological event order $(G_{\mathrm{event}}, \prec)$ is supplied. Global causal consistency can therefore neither be reduced to spatial subgraph prohibitions nor verified solely from local edge inequalities; it must govern the transitive closure $R^+$ directly.

This proven insufficiency imposes a double mandate that drives the final section of Chapter 2. First, irreflexivity of $R^+$ must be instituted explicitly as an independent third foundational postulate (via **Acyclic Effective Causality** <Ref id="2.7.1" label="§2.7.1" />), turning global cyclic closures from admissible countermodels into excluded states. Second, because direct evaluation of unbounded $R^+$-chains conflicts with finite local observation horizons whenever large perimeters evade neighborhood probes, global enforcement must determine both how bounded reverse-witness guards statistically approximate global acyclicity and what thermodynamic coordination and erasure costs attach to any distributed repair.
