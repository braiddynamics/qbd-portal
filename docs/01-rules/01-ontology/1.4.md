## 1.4 Causal Graph {#1.4}

With the manifold removed, only points and connections remain for analysis. Defining a point without a location forces a shift in perspective. It requires that an object must exist solely through its relations to others. If position is not intrinsic, then identity must be derived. A reality where location is defined entirely by connectivity must be constructed. The question arises of how a universe can exist before there is a physical space for it to exist in. Geometry is effectively bootstrapped from pure algebra. This requires abandoning the comfortable intuition of spatial embedding and accepting a world of pure abstraction.

The foundational structure must derive identity entirely from connectivity. The graph is treated as the raw material of spacetime. In this model, the edges themselves carry the burden of causality. Each link represents a transfer of influence. It is a discrete quantum of connection that binds two events together. This web of relations is not a map of the territory; it is the territory itself. It serves as the physical memory of the system, encoding the past interactions that define the present state. Without this rigorous definition, spatial assumptions that undermine the background independence of the theory risk being introduced.

Analysis is restricted to a specific class of graphs to ensure physical viability. Loops where an event serves as its own ancestor are structurally unsound. Vague connections that fail to specify a direction of influence are equally problematic. The necessary components are identified as abstract events and causal links. Logical time is attached to these edges to create a permanent record of creation. By constructing a structure rigid enough to preserve history yet flexible enough to permit evolution, the state space is defined not as a collection of positions but as a collection of relations. This formalization allows the universe to be treated as a mathematical object that can be updated and computed.

### 1.4.1 Definition: Causal Graph Substrate {#1.4.1}

:::tip[**Mathematical Characterization of the Relational Configuration Space as a Formal Architecture**]
:::

Let the **Causal Graph Substrate** at logical tick $t$ be the active spatial configuration $G_t=(V,E_t,H_t)$, where $V$ is a fixed finite vertex set, $E_t\subseteq V\times V$ is irreflexive and asymmetric, and $H_t:E_t\to\mathbb N_0$ records immutable causal heights of the active edge occurrences. Directed spatial cycles are permitted; the chronological order belongs to the distinct creation-event structure.

A creation occurrence is a record $\omega=(u,v,b,h)$ with birth tick $b\in\mathbb N_0$ and causal height $h\in\mathbb N_0$. At most one occurrence of a given ordered pair is created per tick. The active-occurrence map $\alpha_t:E_t\to\mathcal E_t$ identifies the current record and satisfies $H_t(u,v)=h(\alpha_t(u,v))$. The creation ledger $\mathcal E_t$ retains past occurrences after active deletion (**Historical Category** <Ref id="4.1.2" label="§4.1.2" />).

### 1.4.1.1 Commentary: State Space Snapshots {#1.4.1.1}

:::info[**Epistemological Interpretation of the Snapshot Configuration Space**]
:::

Each snapshot $G_t = (V, E_t, H_t)$ encodes the complete relational configuration realized at logical tick $t$ without presupposing continuum coordinates or uncomputed future evolutions. Holding the vertex set finite ($|V| < \infty$) guarantees computational tractability at every step, while tracking creation timestamps through $H_t$ and the historical occurrence map preserves a strict, append-only record of causal precedence.

Unlike static spatial networks in condensed matter physics, a causal graph $G_t$ combines relational spatial connectivity in $E_t$ with an explicit temporal ledger in $H_t$ and the associated creation occurrences $\mathcal{E}_t$. Even as localized topological loops later emerge in active edges, the underlying creation dependence relation remains strictly acyclic. This architecture contrasts with Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDT), which imposes foliated spatial hypersurfaces externally, instead allowing spatial slices and metric intervals to emerge relationally from discrete edge timestamps.

### 1.4.2 Definition: Abstract Event {#1.4.2}

:::tip[**Formal Characterization of Event Vertices as Pre-Geometric Nodes**]
:::

Let $V = \{ v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_N \}$ be a finite set of vertices, where each element $v \in V$ is an **Abstract Event**. An abstract event is a structureless vertex that persists across snapshots and represents the intersection of causal influences. It possesses no intrinsic coordinates, spatial volume, or physical attributes independent of its incidence relations within the edge set $E$.

### 1.4.2.1 Commentary: Pre-Geometric Event Identity {#1.4.2.1}

:::info[**Ontological Justification of Relational Event Identity and Coordinate-Free Spacetime**]
:::

The relational ontology established by **Abstract Event** <Ref id="1.4.2" label="§1.4.2" /> locates identity strictly within the links rather than the nodes. The abstract event diverges fundamentally from a "point" in classical or Riemannian geometry. A geometric point derives identity from extrinsic coordinates embedded within a pre-existing background manifold, which serves as the substantive stage upon which dynamics unfold. In contrast, the abstract event in Quantum Braid Dynamics admits no such background. Its identity emerges purely relationally, defined exhaustively by the directed edges incident to it: outgoing edges designate it as a source, incoming edges as a target, with the degree sequence and timestamp offsets providing the sole descriptors.

The absence of self-attributes ensures that physics originates from the topology and dynamical evolution of the relations interconnecting them. This relational ontology aligns the foundational structure with the background-independent imperatives of quantum gravity theories, where spacetime arises as a derived construct from causal sets or spin networks rather than a primitive arena. The explicit exclusion of coordinates precludes substantivalism, enforcing diffeomorphism invariance at the discrete level: relabeling vertices preserves the causal skeleton, with isomorphism classes under edge-preserving maps defining equivalence. This shift from substantive objects to relational structures not only evades the hole argument but also embeds the theory's discreteness, where events nucleate via edge additions, inheriting timestamps and influences solely from predecessors.

### 1.4.3 Definition: Causal Relation {#1.4.3}

:::tip[**Formal Characterization of Causal Links as Directed Edges in the Active Graph**]
:::

Let $E \subseteq V \times V$ be a set of directed edges, where each ordered pair $e = (u, v) \in E$ is a **Causal Relation**. An edge $e$ represents a direct active link from source vertex $u$ to target vertex $v$. The relation is strictly asymmetric, satisfying:

$$
(u, v) \in E \implies (v, u) \notin E.
$$

### 1.4.3.1 Commentary: Irreducible Influence {#1.4.3.1}

:::info[**Sparsity and Irreducibility of Causal Edge Connections**]
:::

A primitive edge links its endpoints directly. A path through intermediate vertices represents a sequence of links. Chronological precedence orders creation occurrences. Asymmetry excludes reciprocal edges in the same snapshot, and the subset relation $E \subseteq V \times V$ permits sparsity <Cite id="A.17" label="(Bombelli et al., 1987)" />; <Cite id="A.82" label="(Sorkin, 2005)" />, reflecting the vacuum's low density where most potential pairs remain unrealized until relational necessity demands them.

This unmediated edge structure highlights a key distinction between QBD and traditional Causal Set Theory (CST). While CST models spacetime as a continuum Poisson-sprinkled poset under complete transitive closure, QBD maintains explicit, unmediated primitive edges in the active graph and records their creation separately. Within the active graph, physical distance is not an extrinsic coordinate metric, but a relational cost function defined by the minimal path length or rewrite cost required to transfer influence across the network. Geodesic distance thus measures the operational cost of causal propagation, providing a natural bridge between discrete graph topology and emergent metric geometry.

### 1.4.4 Definition: Creation Timestamp {#1.4.4}

:::tip[**Formal Characterization of the Historical Edge Timestamp Mapping as a Formal Architecture**]
:::

Let the **Creation Timestamp** $H_t(e)$ denote the causal height of the active occurrence of $e$, distinct from its scheduler birth tick $b(e)$. For a new edge $e=(u,v)$ inserted during the transition from tick $t$ to tick $t+1$, its height is

$$
h_{\mathrm{new}}(u,v)=1+\max\bigl(\{H_t(x,u):(x,u)\in E_t\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),
\qquad \omega=(u,v,t+1,h_{\mathrm{new}}(u,v)).
$$

Existing occurrences retain their assigned heights. The initial substrate has $H_0(e)=0$. A deleted pair recreated later receives a new occurrence identifier even if its newly computed height equals an earlier height. The birth tick and height are both immutable coordinates of an occurrence; neither is a replacement for the other.

### 1.4.4.1 Commentary: Temporal Discreteness {#1.4.4.1}

:::info[**Thermodynamic and Topological Role of Discretized Creation Markers**]
:::

A creation occurrence carries two temporal coordinates with different roles. Its scheduler birth tick identifies when the parallel update installed the edge. Its causal height records the source-side incoming maximum plus one. Independent regions can acquire equal heights at different ticks, and independent additions can share a tick. Equality of either coordinate alone therefore does not identify events. The ordered pair together with the birth tick identifies a creation in the stated scheduler, while the height provides the strict inequality used on actual predecessor dependencies.

The initialized spatial tree has height zero on every edge. Those simultaneous spatial relations are not chronological successor chains. Subsequent records retain their assigned heights even when the active neighborhood changes. If an edge is deleted and later recreated, its current adjacency value can agree with an earlier snapshot while its occurrence record differs. This separation gives the historical inclusion a precise object to preserve and avoids requiring a spatial vertex to maintain one mutable clock for every past and future use.

### 1.4.6 Lemma: Irreflexivity of Timestamps {#1.4.6}

:::info[**Unsatisfiability of Recursive Timestamp Assignment via Self-Loops**]
:::

Let $e_{self} = (u, u)$ be a self-loop incident to a vertex $u$ in a graph $G$. The recursive timestamp assignment $H(e_{self}) = 1 + \max \left( \{H(e') \mid e' \in \text{In}(u)\} \cup \{0\} \right)$ is inconsistent and admits no stable timestamp assignment.

### 1.4.6.1 Proof: Irreflexivity of Timestamps {#1.4.6.1}

:::tip[**Formal Stability Analysis via Self-Loop Timestamps**]
:::

**I. Fixed-Point Equation for a Self-Loop**

Let $e_*=(u,u)$ and suppose a stable timestamp $h=H(e_*)\in\mathbb N_0$ satisfies the stated recurrence (**Irreflexivity of Timestamps** <Ref id="1.4.6" label="§1.4.6" />). Put

$$
A=\{H(e'):e'\in\operatorname{In}(u)\}\cup\{0\},\qquad
m=\max A,\qquad h=1+m
$$

with the timestamp rule fixed above (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />). The self-loop is incoming to $u$, because its target is $u$:

$$
e_*=(u,u)\in\operatorname{In}(u)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h\in A.
$$

**II. Maximum Bound and Contradiction**

Membership in $A$ gives $h\le m$. Substituting $m=h-1$ from the recurrence gives

$$
h\le m=h-1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\le-1,
$$

which is false. Equivalently,

$$
h=1+m\ge1+h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h-h\ge1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad0\ge1.
$$

The contradiction applies to every candidate finite value $h$.

**III. Pre-Insertion Value and Re-Evaluation**

Let $m_0$ be the maximum on the incoming edges before insertion. A provisional assignment $h_0=m_0+1$ gives the new incoming maximum

$$
m_1=\max\{m_0,h_0\}=h_0,
$$

because $h_0>m_0$. Re-evaluation of the same recurrence then requires

$$
h_1=1+m_1=h_0+1\ne h_0.
$$

The post-insertion update map is $\Psi(h)=1+\max\{m_0,h\}$. Its increment satisfies

$$
\Psi(h)-h=
\begin{cases}
1+m_0-h\ge1,&h\le m_0,\\
1+h-h=1,&h>m_0.
\end{cases}
$$

The iteration formula $h_j=m_0+j+1$ holds at $j=0$. If it holds at $j$, then

$$
\max\{m_0,h_j\}=h_j,\qquad h_{j+1}=1+h_j=m_0+(j+1)+1.
$$

Thus no finite provisional value is a fixed point:

$$
\boxed{e_*\in\operatorname{In}(u)\ \Longrightarrow\
\nexists h\in\mathbb N_0:\ h=1+\max\bigl(\{H(e'):e'\in\operatorname{In}(u),\ e'\ne e_*\}\cup\{0,h\}\bigr).}
$$

Q.E.D.

### 1.4.6.2 Commentary: Loop Exclusion {#1.4.6.2}

:::info[**Logical and Topological Prevention of Grandfather Paradoxes**]
:::

The logical exclusion of self-loops represents the atomic prevention of closed timelike curves. In general relativity, closed timelike curves permit retroactive causal influence, leading to logical inconsistencies. In QBD's pre-geometric framework, the stability contradiction ensures that no event can serve as its own cause, enforcing strict irreflexivity.

This structure maintains a distinction between the dependency graph of creation occurrences and the active spatial graph $G_t$. Each dependency step strictly increases causal height, so a nonempty chain returning to its starting occurrence would require that occurrence's height to exceed itself (**Monotonicity of History** <Ref id="1.4.9" label="§1.4.9" />). A directed triangle closes a circuit among spatial vertices. Its three active edges do not impose a closed chain of dependencies among their creation occurrences.

The contradiction concerns a stable assignment satisfying the recurrence on the graph containing the proposed self-loop. A provisional value computed from the pre-insertion graph is not itself such a stable assignment. Using this algebraic obstruction as a constructor rejection rule therefore requires the constructor to enforce the stated consistency condition, or to reject self-loops directly under the irreflexive causal-link constraint. The calculation does not equate an unchecked provisional timestamp with a successful validation.

### 1.4.7 Lemma: Transitive Causal Monotonicity {#1.4.7}

:::info[**Monotonic Timestamp Progression along Directed Causal Chains by Inductive Path Extension**]
:::

Let $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ be a sequentially instantiated causal chain whose occurrence of $e_i$ remains incoming to the source when $e_{i+1}$ is created, for each $i<k$. Then $H(e_1)<\cdots<H(e_k)$, and for $k\ge2$ the endpoint bound is $H(e_k)\ge H(e_1)+k-1$ (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />).

### 1.4.7.1 Proof: Transitive Causal Monotonicity {#1.4.7.1}
 
:::tip[**Inductive Demonstration via Strict Timestamp Increase**]
:::

**I. Predecessor Membership at Creation**

Let $\omega_i$ be the occurrence of $e_i$ and put $h_i=h(\omega_i)$. The sequential-chain hypothesis means that, when $e_{i+1}$ is created from a snapshot $G_{t_i}$, its source is the target of $e_i$ and $e_i$ is still present there. Consequently

$$
h_i\in\{H_{t_i}(x,s(e_{i+1})):(x,s(e_{i+1}))\in E_{t_i}\}.
$$

**II. Consecutive Inequality**

The source-side recurrence (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />) therefore gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{i+1}
&=1+\max\bigl(\{H_{t_i}(x,s(e_{i+1}))\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)\\
&\ge1+h_i>h_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here the set in the maximum ranges over the incoming edges of that source in the creation snapshot. Both the membership and the maximum refer to that same snapshot.

**III. Induction on the Chain**

For $j=1$, $h_j=h_1=h_1+j-1$. If $h_j\ge h_1+j-1$, then

$$
h_{j+1}\ge h_j+1\ge h_1+j=h_1+(j+1)-1.
$$

Induction proves $h_j\ge h_1+j-1$ for all $j\le k$. For $1\le i<j\le k$, the consecutive increments telescope:

$$
h_j-h_i=\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}(h_{\ell+1}-h_\ell)\ge\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}1=j-i>0.
$$

Thus $h_i<h_j$ for every ordered pair of distinct positions. This establishes the sequential-chain claim (**Transitive Causal Monotonicity** <Ref id="1.4.7" label="§1.4.7" />).

**IV. Conclusion**

$$
\boxed{h_1<\cdots<h_k,\qquad h_k\ge h_1+k-1.}
$$

Q.E.D.

### 1.4.7.2 Commentary: Lamport Ordering {#1.4.7.2}

:::info[**Clock Synchronization and Topological Arrow of Time**]
:::

Sequential causality and spatial adjacency carry different information. A later incoming edge can meet an older outgoing edge at the same spatial vertex without making the latter a causal successor. Its immutable height is not recomputed. In a closed spatial triangle this timestamp drop blocks continuation around the perimeter as a strictly increasing chronological chain. The spatial cycle and the acyclic dependency order can consequently coexist without identifying their vertex domains or requiring the active graph to be a directed acyclic graph.

The chain hypothesis records both succession and predecessor presence at the instant of creation. It is this snapshot membership that justifies the maximum inequality in the proof. A spatial walk assembled from edges created in another order has no such implication. Heights increase along recorded dependencies, following the ordering condition for Lamport's logical clocks <Cite id="A.53" label="(Lamport, 1978)" />. Scheduler birth ticks distinguish repeated creations and identify the parallel update index. These two coordinates together permit a precise account of causal ancestry without changing old outgoing heights whenever the spatial incidence pattern is modified.

### 1.4.8 Lemma: Least Admissible Causal Height {#1.4.8}

:::info[**Existence of a Pointwise Least Admissible Labeling from the Source-Maximum Recurrence**]
:::

Let an integer labeling $c$ of the creation occurrences be admissible if its values are at least zero on initial occurrences and at least one on later occurrences, and $c(\eta)\ge c(\omega)+1$ whenever $\omega D\eta$.
Then the source-maximum height with zero initialization is the pointwise least admissible labeling (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />).

### 1.4.8.1 Proof: Least Admissible Causal Height {#1.4.8.1}

:::tip[**Pointwise Comparison by Induction on Birth Tick**]
:::

**I. Direct Predecessors and the Source-Maximum Formula**

Let $P(\eta)$ denote the set $\{\omega:\omega D\eta\}$ of direct predecessors of an occurrence $\eta$. An initial occurrence has birth tick zero and height zero (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />). A direct predecessor must be present in the input snapshot of the transition creating its successor. The initial snapshot is stipulated, which implies that no transition creates its initial occurrences. The dependency definition then yields $P(\eta)=\varnothing$ when $b(\eta)=0$.

For a later occurrence $\eta=(u,v,n,h(\eta))$ with $n\ge1$, the transition creating it uses $G_{n-1}$. Let $I_\eta$ denote the set of incoming pairs of its source in that snapshot. By the definition of $D$, a predecessor of $\eta$ is precisely the active occurrence of one such pair. The active-occurrence map yields (**Causal Graph Substrate** <Ref id="1.4.1" label="§1.4.1" />)

$$
I_\eta=\{(x,u)\in E_{n-1}:x\in V\},
\qquad
P(\eta)=\{\alpha_{n-1}(x,u):(x,u)\in I_\eta\}.
$$

The common second coordinate $u$ implies that distinct pairs in $I_\eta$ have distinct first coordinates. Projection onto the first coordinate is injective into the finite set $V$. The image under $\alpha_{n-1}$ has at most one entry per pair in $I_\eta$, which implies $|P(\eta)|\le |I_\eta|\le |V|$. Activity of each $\omega\in P(\eta)$ at tick $n-1$ implies that it was born by that tick and yields

$$
b(\omega)\le n-1<n=b(\eta).
$$

For every $(x,u)\in I_\eta$, the equality $H_{n-1}(x,u)=h(\alpha_{n-1}(x,u))$ follows from the definition of the active height. Substitution into the source-maximum rule and use of the displayed description of $P(\eta)$ yield

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(\eta)
&=1+\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{H_{n-1}(x,u):(x,u)\in I_\eta\}\bigr)\\
&=1+\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{h(\alpha_{n-1}(x,u)):(x,u)\in I_\eta\}\bigr)\\
&=1+\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{h(\omega):\omega\in P(\eta)\}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $S_\eta$ denote $\{0\}\cup\{h(\omega):\omega\in P(\eta)\}$, and let $M_\eta$ denote $\max S_\eta$. Finiteness of $S_\eta$ and membership of zero imply that $M_\eta$ belongs to $S_\eta$. For every $a\in S_\eta$, $a\le M_\eta$ implies $a+1\le M_\eta+1$. Membership of $M_\eta$ in $S_\eta$ implies membership of $M_\eta+1$ in the shifted set $\{a+1:a\in S_\eta\}$. The attained upper bound yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(\eta)
&=1+M_\eta\\
&=\max\{a+1:a\in S_\eta\}\\
&=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{h(\omega)+1:\omega\in P(\eta)\}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality uses the shift of $0$ to $1$ and the shift of each predecessor height to $h(\omega)+1$.

**II. Verification of the Admissibility Conditions**

At birth tick zero, $h(\eta)=0$ and $0\ge0$ establish the initial floor $h(\eta)\ge0$. At every later tick, membership of $1$ in the set whose maximum equals $h(\eta)$ implies $h(\eta)\ge1$.

If $\omega D\eta$, then $\omega\in P(\eta)$, and $\eta$ is a later occurrence. Membership of $h(\omega)+1$ in the same set yields

$$
h(\eta)=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{h(\xi)+1:\xi\in P(\eta)\}\bigr)
\ge h(\omega)+1.
$$

The initial floor, the later floor, and the direct-dependency inequality establish admissibility of $h$.

**III. Pointwise Comparison by Strong Induction**

Let $c$ denote an arbitrary admissible labeling. For each $n\in\mathbb N_0$, the induction assertion is

$$
\forall\eta\quad b(\eta)=n\ \Longrightarrow\ c(\eta)\ge h(\eta).
$$

At $n=0$, initialization implies $h(\eta)=0$ for every occurrence with $b(\eta)=0$. The initial floor for $c$ yields

$$
c(\eta)\ge0=h(\eta).
$$

Let $n$ denote a positive birth tick. Assume the assertion for every birth tick $j<n$. Let $\eta$ denote an arbitrary occurrence with $b(\eta)=n$. The later floor for $c$ implies $c(\eta)\ge1$. For any $\omega\in P(\eta)$, let $j$ denote $b(\omega)$. The comparison from Part I establishes $j<n$. The induction hypothesis at $j$ yields $c(\omega)\ge h(\omega)$. Adding one to both sides and using admissibility across $\omega D\eta$ yields

$$
c(\eta)\ge c(\omega)+1\ge h(\omega)+1 .
$$

Every member of $\{1\}\cup\{h(\omega)+1:\omega\in P(\eta)\}$ is bounded by $c(\eta)$ from above. Its maximum belongs to the set and equals $h(\eta)$. The bound on that member establishes $c(\eta)\ge h(\eta)$ at tick $n$. When there are no predecessors, the set is $\{1\}$ and the same bound yields $c(\eta)\ge1=h(\eta)$.

If the assertion failed at any tick, its failing ticks would form a nonempty subset of $\mathbb N_0$ with a least element. The base case excludes zero. At any positive least failing tick, the assertion holds at every smaller tick. The induction step then implies that the assertion holds at the supposed failing tick. This contradiction establishes the assertion at every birth tick.

**IV. Least Admissible Labeling**

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the set of admissible labelings. Membership $h\in\mathcal C$ follows from Part II. Part III applies to each $c\in\mathcal C$, and every occurrence has a birth tick in $\mathbb N_0$. These facts yield

$$
h\in\mathcal C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\forall c\in\mathcal C\ \forall\eta,\quad h(\eta)\le c(\eta).
$$

These are the membership and comparison conditions for $h$ to be the pointwise least element of $\mathcal C$.

Q.E.D.

### 1.4.8.2 Commentary: Causal Height and Scheduler Delay {#1.4.8.2}

:::info[**Invariance of Least Causal Heights under Dependency-Preserving Rescheduling**]
:::

The birth tick places an occurrence in a particular execution. Its causal height records the integer constraints imposed by the occurrences on which it depends. A global delay can move an occurrence to a later tick while preserving both its predecessors and its initial or later status. When those data are preserved throughout the history, the admissibility conditions are identical, so the pointwise least heights coincide (**Least Admissible Causal Height** <Ref id="1.4.8" label="§1.4.8" />).

The preservation condition matters when updates are rescheduled. Moving a creation across a deletion can change which incoming occurrences are present in its pre-tick snapshot. A later insertion of the same edge can also replace a predecessor by a distinct occurrence, even if the active adjacency looks the same. The fixed dependency relation and birth floors therefore determine what the height records. Changes to those inputs require a new evaluation of the recurrence.

### 1.4.9 Proof: Monotonicity of History {#1.4.9}

:::tip[**Synthesis of Irreflexivity via Transitivity to Establish Global Acyclicity**]
:::

**I. Direct Event Dependencies**

Let $\omega D\eta$ be a dependency with the stipulated creation-snapshot meaning (**Monotonicity of History** <Ref id="1.4.5" label="§1.4.5" />). The preceding incoming occurrence belongs to the creation snapshot of $\eta$. The source-side maximum therefore gives

$$
\omega D\eta\ \Longrightarrow\ h(\eta)\ge h(\omega)+1.
$$

Stable self-loop assignment also has the independent obstruction $h\ge h+1$ (**Irreflexivity of Timestamps** <Ref id="1.4.6" label="§1.4.6" />). Physical self-loops are excluded directly by the irreflexive edge constraint.

The source-maximum assignment is the pointwise least admissible labeling (**Least Admissible Causal Height** <Ref id="1.4.8" label="§1.4.8" />).

**II. Finite Dependency Paths**

A witness for $\omega D^+\eta$ is a chain $\omega=\omega_0D\omega_1D\cdots D\omega_m=\eta$ with $m\ge1$. The sequential inequality (**Transitive Causal Monotonicity** <Ref id="1.4.7" label="§1.4.7" />) gives

$$
h(\eta)\ge h(\omega)+m>h(\omega).
$$

**III. Irreflexivity and Transitivity**

If $\omega D^+\omega$, the endpoint inequality becomes $h(\omega)>h(\omega)$, a contradiction. If $\omega D^+\eta$ and $\eta D^+\zeta$, concatenation of the two finite dependency chains is a nonempty dependency chain from $\omega$ to $\zeta$. Thus

$$
\neg(\omega D^+\omega),\qquad
(\omega D^+\eta\land\eta D^+\zeta)\Longrightarrow\omega D^+\zeta.
$$

**IV. Well-Founded Height Descent**

A reversed dependency chain $\omega_{j+1}D^+\omega_j$ satisfies $h(\omega_{j+1})\le h(\omega_j)-1$. Induction gives $h(\omega_j)+j\le h(\omega_0)$. Taking $j=h(\omega_0)+1$ contradicts nonnegativity. Therefore no infinite descending dependency chain exists, and $D^+$ is a well-founded strict partial order on creation occurrences.

Q.E.D.

### 1.4.Z Implications and Synthesis {#1.4.Z}

:::note[**Causal Graph Substrate**]
:::

An explicit network of directed relations replaces every presupposed coordinate grid in the **Causal Graph Substrate** <Ref id="1.4.1" label="§1.4.1" />. Because every newly added link carries a monotonic causal height $H_t(e)$ alongside its scheduler birth tick $b(e)$, the **Irreflexivity of Timestamps** <Ref id="1.4.6" label="§1.4.6" /> bars self-loops cleanly at the level of edge creation. Causality thereby assumes a concrete combinatorial form whose local ordering is legible directly from graph incidence.

This structure functions simultaneously as geometry and archival memory across transitions. Historical records encode past **Causal Relation** <Ref id="1.4.3" label="§1.4.3" /> states while preserving strict partial ordering through the **Monotonicity of History** <Ref id="1.4.5" label="§1.4.5" />. Spatial separation is measured purely by relational path length rather than distances across an external void, and embedding causal heights $H_t(e)$ onto active edges stores dynamical succession directly inside topology without violating snapshot finiteness.

With vertices, directed links, and timestamp monotonicity formalized, the substrate itself is complete as a sequence of well-typed configurations. Yet a sequence of graph definitions specifies only permissible states, not the elementary transitions linking one snapshot to the next. Physics requires legal local operations capable of constructing and dissolving causal relations without corrupting historical consistency. We turn next to the elementary task space governing valid graph updates.
