## 1.5 Task Space {#1.5}

Operations on the graph cannot be arbitrary because they must be rigidly constrained by physical necessity. If the substrate were allowed to mutate without restriction, a universe with infinite degrees of freedom would result. This would lack the continuity required for the emergence of persistent physical laws. The absolute minimum set of operations capable of transforming one state into another while preserving the discrete integrity of the events themselves must therefore be identified. Nodes cannot simply appear or disappear at random. The transformation must be continuous and conservative to maintain the coherence of physical objects over time.

The fundamental symmetry between the act of forging a connection and the act of severing it is explored. A balance is sought that permits the universe to breathe by expanding and contracting its relational web without requiring an external architect to direct every change. A mechanism must be found that allows complexity to arise from simplicity. Only local operations that do not require knowledge of the global state must be used. This constraint ensures that physics remains local and causal. It prevents "spooky action at a distance" from being baked into the fundamental rules. The mechanism must be blind to the whole, acting only on the immediate neighborhood.

Admissible transformations are restricted to a Task Space containing only those moves that are kinematically possible. A vast repertoire of complex actions is unnecessary because a minimal set of primitive operations suffices to describe all possible evolutions. The additive process, which increases the relational density, is distinguished from the subtractive process, which prunes it. These operations stand as inverses to one another. This ensures that the fundamental dynamics remain reversible in principle, even if the statistical behavior of the system eventually renders them irreversible in practice.

### 1.5.1 Definition: Elementary Task Space {#1.5.1}

:::tip[**Mathematical Characterization of the Admissible Transformation Space as a Formal Architecture**]
:::

Let the **Elementary Task Space** $\mathfrak T(G)$ consist of guarded additions and deletions on a fixed spatial vertex set $V$. An addition changes one absent directed pair to present, preserves irreflexivity and asymmetry, assigns the source-side causal height, and satisfies the applicable candidate-relative PUC and causal-admission predicates; a deletion removes one present edge without erasing its historical occurrence (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />). The uniqueness predicate is candidate-relative (**Principle of Unique Causality (PUC)** <Ref id="2.3.4" label="§2.3.4" />).

Each primitive preserves $V$, changes at most one active edge, and leaves all surviving occurrence heights unchanged. Spatial triangle creation is permitted. The event dependency order, rather than all active spatial adjacency, is the chronological structure. A fixed-snapshot parallel merge and a sequential re-evaluation of guards are different task schedules.

### 1.5.1.1 Commentary: Kinematic Purity and Independence {#1.5.1.1}

:::info[**Separation of Kinematic Feasibility from Dynamical Weighting**]
:::

A defining virtue of this task-theoretic formulation resides in its kinematic purity: membership in $\mathfrak{T}$ invokes no oracle of probability, no calculus of free energy, nor any measure of dynamical preferability. The space enumerates merely the structural feasibility of flux, remaining agnostic to enactment frequency or energetic toll. An addition $\mathfrak{T}_{add}(u,v)$ qualifies if irreflexive and compliant with the **Monotonicity of History**, but its thermodynamic viability ($\Delta F < 0$ at vacuum temperature) defers to the **Addition Mode** <Ref id="4.5.3" label="§4.5.3" />. Deletions preserve $H$'s monotonicity yet postpone Landauer costs until **Deletion Mode** <Ref id="4.5.4" label="§4.5.4" /> is active.
 
 Within this space, transformations must not invoke infinite resources, permit retroactive revisions to timestamps, or violate the irreflexive causal primitive defined by **Directed Causal Link** <Ref id="2.1.1" label="§2.1.1" />. The preservation of acyclicity ensures that the target graph $G'$ admits no directed cycles, enforcing **Acyclic Effective Causality**. Monotonicity of $H$ requires that new timestamps exceed predecessors, which aligns with **Monotonicity of History**, and finite growth bounds $|V'| \leq |V| + k$ preventing unbounded structural blooms. Independent of probabilistic weighting or energetic viability, $\mathfrak{T}$ enumerates exhaustively "what can be built" from the discrete relations, serving as the kinematic substrate upon which dynamical laws impose selection <Cite id="A.58" label="(Abramsky, 2023)" />.
 
 This stratification upholds **Coherentist Justification**. Ontology affords the task space, while axioms constrain its potential to the **Principle of Unique Causality (PUC)** <Ref id="2.3.4" label="§2.3.4" />. Finally, the dynamics impose the **Universal Constructor** <Ref id="4.5.1" label="§4.5.1" />. The vacuum's relationality thus emerges as the agent of becoming: persistent yet enabling the full cycle of construction that begets the universe from nullity. This independence ensures modularity: alterations to dynamical parameters (e.g., temperature scaling) perturb selection without reshaping kinematic possibility, facilitating isolation of ontology from mechanism and permitting the theory's scalability across regimes.

### 1.5.2 Definition: Edge Addition Task {#1.5.2}

:::tip[**Formal Specification of the Primitive Edge Insertion Operator via Edge Addition Task**]
:::

Let the **Edge Addition Task** $\mathfrak T_{add}(u,v)$ act on $G_t=(V,E_t,H_t)$ for an absent pair $(u,v)$ with $u\ne v$ and $(v,u)\notin E_t$. Its admitted candidate includes the required geometric and causal guard evidence. The updated components are

$$
V'=V,\qquad E'=E_t\cup\{(u,v)\},\qquad
H'|_{E_t}=H_t,\qquad
H'(u,v)=1+\max\bigl(\{H_t(x,u):(x,u)\in E_t\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).
$$

The transition from tick $t$ records the new occurrence $(u,v,t+1,H'(u,v))$. For a return chord across $v\to w\to u$, PUC is evaluated relative to that candidate (**Principle of Unique Causality (PUC)** <Ref id="2.3.4" label="§2.3.4" />). Passing PUC is one guard, not an assertion that every other guard holds. The target spatial graph can contain the newly closed triangle.

### 1.5.2.1 Commentary: Causal Construction {#1.5.2.1}

:::info[**Accretion of Causal Links and Relational Horizon Expansion**]
:::

The edge addition task $\mathfrak{T}_{add}(u, v)$ represents the primitive creation operator that accretes a novel causal link $e = (u, v)$ with an emergent timestamp $H(e) = t_L$. This task instantiates an unmediated causal proposition, extending the local relational horizon and establishing a direct connection between previously unlinked event loci. In the early stages of network evolution, edge additions predominate, driving the growth of the causal graph and kindling relational density from primordial sparsity.

By connecting an existing vertex pair, the edge addition task creates the structural preconditions for spatial geometry. For instance, adding a link across an open 2-path closes the triangular motif, instantiating a 3-cycle (**Geometric Quantum** <Ref id="2.3.3" label="§2.3.3" />) that defines an elementary unit of spatial area. Edge addition thus serves as the kinematic engine that builds 3D spatial hypersurfaces and 4D causal connectivity from fundamental relational steps.

### 1.5.3 Definition: Edge Deletion Task {#1.5.3}

:::tip[**Formal Specification of the Primitive Edge Excision Operator via Edge Deletion Task**]
:::

Let $G = (V, E, H)$ be a causal graph. For any edge $e = (u, v) \in E$, the **Edge Deletion Task** $\mathfrak{T}_{del}(u, v)$ is the mapping:

$$
\mathfrak{T}_{del}(u, v): G \mapsto G' = (V', E', H')
$$

where the target components are defined by:
1.  **Vertex Set**: $V' = V$.
2.  **Edge Set**: $E' = E \setminus \{(u, v)\}$.
3.  **Timestamp Assignment**: $H'$ is the restriction of $H$ to $E'$, satisfying $H'(e') = H(e')$ for all $e' \in E'$.

### 1.5.3.1 Commentary: Causal Destruction {#1.5.3.1}

:::info[**Excision of Causal Links and Historical Poset Monotonicity**]
:::

The edge deletion task $\mathfrak{T}_{del}(u, v)$ executes the primitive excision transformation $G \to G - e$, where $e = (u, v) \in E$, removing an active link while preserving the immutable historical timestamp $H(e)$ and maintaining the global acyclicity of the target graph $G'$. This operation contracts superfluous connections and resolves local topological tensions, pruning redundant paths to enforce structural parsimony under **The Deletion Probability** <Ref id="4.5.7" label="§4.5.7" />.

$\mathfrak{T}_{del}$ is defined as a topological modification, not an informational erasure. Within the Elementary Task Space, the excision of a causal link $e$ removes the *active relation* (causal influence) but does not retroactively annihilate the *event of its creation*. The task space assumes an "Append-Only" metaphysics regarding the Global Sequencer's log: $t_L$ at which $e$ was created remains a persistent property of the universe's trajectory, even if the geometric constituent $e$ is removed from the active graph $G$. This distinction allows for the pruning of geometry without the paradox of altering the past. Critically, this append-only historical poset complies with the **Monotonicity of History** <Ref id="1.4.5" label="§1.4.5" /> while incurring zero runtime memory overhead. When $e$ is pruned by $\mathfrak{T}_{del}$ (as established by the **Vacuum Repertoire** <Ref id="1.5.4" label="§1.5.4" />), it is fully excised from the active state graph data structure, maintaining strict structural sparsity and computational efficiency without retaining inactive or historically deleted edges in memory.

### 1.5.5 Lemma: Relational Vertex Emergence {#1.5.5}

:::info[**Subordination via Vertex Existence to Edge Topology**]
:::

Let $G = (V, E, H)$ be a causal graph, and let $V_{act} = \{ v \in V \mid \exists u \in V \text{ such that } (u, v) \in E \lor (v, u) \in E \}$ be the active vertex set. The creation or destruction of a vertex is strictly subordinate to edge operations, with no primitive task in $\mathfrak{T}_{vac}$ directly mutating the vertex set $V$.

### 1.5.5.1 Proof: Relational Vertex Emergence {#1.5.5.1}

:::tip[**Verification of Vertex Subordination through Primitive Operations**]
:::

**I. Primitive State Components**

Let $G=(V,E,H)$ and $e=(u,v)$ with $u\ne v$. The two primitive definitions give

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{primitive}&V'&E'\\\hline
\mathfrak T_{add}(u,v)&V&E\cup\{e\}\\
\mathfrak T_{del}(u,v)&V&E\setminus\{e\}
\end{array}
$$

(**Edge Addition Task** <Ref id="1.5.2" label="§1.5.2" />), (**Edge Deletion Task** <Ref id="1.5.3" label="§1.5.3" />). Hence $V'=V$ in both cases.

**II. Incidence Change Under Addition**

Define $d_G(x)=d_{in,G}(x)+d_{\mathrm{out},G}(x)$ and $V_{act}(G)=\{x:d_G(x)>0\}$. For a legal addition, $e\notin E$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{out},G'}(x)&=d_{\mathrm{out},G}(x)+\mathbf1_{\{u\}}(x),\\
d_{in,G'}(x)&=d_{in,G}(x)+\mathbf1_{\{v\}}(x),\\
d_{G'}(x)&=d_G(x)+\mathbf1_{\{u\}}(x)+\mathbf1_{\{v\}}(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Every previously positive degree remains positive. Each endpoint gains one, and every other degree is unchanged. Therefore

$$
V_{act}(G')=V_{act}(G)\cup\{u,v\}.
$$

The formula allows either endpoint already to be active; set union adds no duplicate element.

**III. Incidence Change Under Deletion**

For a legal deletion, $e\in E$, so both endpoints have degree at least one. The corresponding equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{out},G'}(x)&=d_{\mathrm{out},G}(x)-\mathbf1_{\{u\}}(x),\\
d_{in,G'}(x)&=d_{in,G}(x)-\mathbf1_{\{v\}}(x),\\
d_{G'}(x)&=d_G(x)-\mathbf1_{\{u\}}(x)-\mathbf1_{\{v\}}(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $u\ne v$, each endpoint loses exactly one. An endpoint leaves the active set exactly when its old total degree was one:

$$
V_{act}(G')=V_{act}(G)\setminus
\{x\in\{u,v\}:d_G(x)=1\}.
$$

**IV. Finite Primitive Sequences**

For $G_{j+1}=T_j(G_j)$ with $T_j\in\mathfrak T_{vac}$, the base equality is $V(G_0)=V$. The single-step identity gives

$$
V(G_j)=V\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V(G_{j+1})=V(G_j)=V.
$$

Induction establishes $V(G_m)=V$ for every finite $m$. The active set is recalculated from the displayed incidence equations at each step, which proves the stated subordination (**Relational Vertex Emergence** <Ref id="1.5.5" label="§1.5.5" />).

$$
\boxed{V(G_m)=V(G_0),\qquad
V_{act}(G_j)=\{x\in V:d_{G_j}(x)>0\}.}
$$

Q.E.D.

### 1.5.5.2 Commentary: Ontological Minimality {#1.5.5.2}

:::info[**Ontological Significance of Vertex Subordination**]
:::

By subordinating the existence of vertices strictly to the incidence of directed edges, the theory enforces a radical ontological minimality. Vertices are not independent, pre-existing physical objects that float freely in a background space; they exist exclusively as the endpoints of active causal relations. This formulation eliminates the need for an independent vertex-creation mechanism, ensuring that space, matter, and event loci emerge purely from the accretion and pruning of relational connections.

This relational subordination guarantees that empty space cannot exist as an autonomous substance. A vertex deprived of all incident edges ceases to participate in the active state graph, carrying zero structural weight in the task space. Consequently, spatial volume and physical entity identity are constructed from the bottom up out of network topology, preventing non-relational substantivalism from polluting the substrate.

The result distinguishes the fixed ambient vertex set of this primitive repertoire from the vertices currently supported by at least one edge. Activation and isolation alter incidence status within that ambient set. Neither operation creates a new vertex label or proves an extension of the ambient set to arbitrary graph sizes. A variable-vertex model therefore needs a separately specified state-space construction; it cannot be inferred solely from an endpoint becoming active.

### 1.5.6 Lemma: Reversibility of Primitives {#1.5.6}

:::info[**Reversibility of Primitives via Partial Inverses on Active Adjacency and Heights**]
:::

Let a snapshot have adjacency $E(x)\in\{0,1\}$ and height $H(x)\in\mathbb N$ at every directed pair $x$, with height zero when absent. Both fields are restored by adding an absent pair and then deleting it, or by deleting an active pair and then restoring it at its saved height.

### 1.5.6.1 Proof: Reversibility of Primitives {#1.5.6.1}

:::tip[**Reversibility of Primitives via Pointwise Inversion of Edge Updates**]
:::

**I. Update Maps on a Fixed Directed-Pair Domain**

Write $G=(E,H)$ in the active-field representation (**Creation Timestamp** <Ref id="1.4.4" label="§1.4.4" />), with $H(x)=0$ on absent pairs. Fix $e$. If $S_G(x)=(E_G(x),H_G(x))$, the addition and deletion maps are defined pointwise by

$$
S_{A_{e,h}(G)}(x)=
\begin{cases}(1,h),&x=e,\\S_G(x),&x\ne e,\end{cases}
\qquad
S_{D_e(G)}(x)=
\begin{cases}(0,0),&x=e,\\S_G(x),&x\ne e.\end{cases}
$$

**II. Addition Followed by Deletion**

Suppose $E_G(e)=0$. At the edited pair and away from it, respectively,

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{D_e(A_{e,h}(G))}(e)&=(0,0)=S_G(e),\\
S_{D_e(A_{e,h}(G))}(x)&=S_{A_{e,h}(G)}(x)=S_G(x)
&& (x\ne e).
\end{aligned}
$$

Both coordinates agree at every pair.

**III. Deletion Followed by Recorded Restoration**

Suppose $E_G(e)=1$ and save $h_0=H_G(e)$. The two compositions at the edited pair and every other pair are

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{A_{e,h_0}(D_e(G))}(e)&=(1,h_0)=(E_G(e),H_G(e)),\\
S_{A_{e,h_0}(D_e(G))}(x)&=S_{D_e(G)}(x)=S_G(x)
&& (x\ne e).
\end{aligned}
$$

Extensional equality of the two active fields gives the partial inverses (**Reversibility of Primitives** <Ref id="1.5.6" label="§1.5.6" />)

$$
\boxed{D_e(A_{e,h}(G))=G\quad(E_G(e)=0),\qquad
A_{e,H_G(e)}(D_e(G))=G\quad(E_G(e)=1).}
$$

Q.E.D.

### 1.5.6.2 Commentary: Constructor Reversibility {#1.5.6.2}

:::info[**Recorded Restoration and Chronological Evolution**]
:::

An active deletion keeps enough information for the pointwise inverse when the old edge and its height are saved. The restoration writes the recorded height directly. A fresh addition at the same ordered pair instead computes its height from the present input graph. The two operations can therefore agree on adjacency and differ in their height fields.

The historical record distinguishes occurrences even when the active fields are restored. An implementation that needs reversible bookkeeping can retain the edited pair and its old height as part of its record. The chronology of fresh constructor actions remains governed by the creation rule. Reading the inverse as an identity on active fields makes its scope explicit and keeps the interpretation of the occurrence ledger separate.

### 1.5.7 Proof: Vacuum Repertoire {#1.5.7}

:::tip[**Vacuum Repertoire via Composition of Pointwise Rollbacks**]
:::

**I. Domain and Objects**

A task in the space specifies an admissible trajectory of active snapshots (**Vacuum Repertoire** <Ref id="1.5.4" label="§1.5.4" />) (**Elementary Task Space** <Ref id="1.5.1" label="§1.5.1" />).

**II. Finite Task Composition**

An admissible task has a witness sequence $G_0\xrightarrow{T_1}G_1\to\cdots\xrightarrow{T_m}G_m$, with each $T_j$ a guarded primitive in its input snapshot. Thus $G_m=(T_m\circ\cdots\circ T_1)(G_0)$. Each step preserves $V$, and at index $j$ the active vertex set is $\{v\in V:\deg_{G_j}(v)>0\}$ (**Relational Vertex Emergence** <Ref id="1.5.5" label="§1.5.5" />).

**III. Recorded Rollback**

Let $R_j$ be the restoration determined by the record for step $j$. The pointwise identities give $R_j(G_j)=G_{j-1}$ (**Reversibility of Primitives** <Ref id="1.5.6" label="§1.5.6" />). Substitution from the last index to the first gives

$$
(R_1\circ\cdots\circ R_m)(G_m)=
(R_1\circ\cdots\circ R_{m-1})(G_{m-1})=\cdots=G_0 .
$$

Q.E.D.

### 1.5.8 Type-Theoretic Validation via Lean 4 Core {#1.5.8}

:::note[**Lean 4 Encoding of Recorded Edge Rollback via Pointwise Equality**]
:::

Type-theoretic certification of the active-field rollback identities (**Reversibility of Primitives** <Ref id="1.5.6" label="§1.5.6" />) and their pointwise derivation (**Reversibility of Primitives** <Ref id="1.5.6.1" label="§1.5.6.1" />) proceeds via the following verification strategy.

1.  **Encoding:** A timestamped graph stores a Boolean adjacency and a natural height at each directed pair.
2.  **Theorem Statements:** The two compositions preserve both fields at every pair in their stated domains.
3.  **Proof Closure:** The proof splits on equality with the edited pair and rewrites the corresponding conditionals.

```lean
structure TimestampedGraph (V : Type) [DecidableEq V] where
  E : V × V → Bool
  H : V × V → Nat

def T_add {V : Type} [DecidableEq V] (e : V × V) (h_rec : Nat) (G : TimestampedGraph V) :
    TimestampedGraph V :=
  { E := fun x => if x = e then true else G.E x
    H := fun x => if x = e then h_rec else G.H x }

def T_del {V : Type} [DecidableEq V] (e : V × V) (G : TimestampedGraph V) :
    TimestampedGraph V × Nat :=
  ( { E := fun x => if x = e then false else G.E x
      H := fun x => if x = e then 0 else G.H x },
    G.H e )

theorem del_then_recorded_add_pointwise {V : Type} [DecidableEq V]
    (G : TimestampedGraph V) (e x : V × V) (h_act : G.E e = true) :
    (T_add e (T_del e G).2 (T_del e G).1).E x = G.E x ∧
    (T_add e (T_del e G).2 (T_del e G).1).H x = G.H x := by
  dsimp [T_add, T_del]
  by_cases hx : x = e
  · subst hx
    constructor
    · rw [ite_eq_left rfl]
      exact h_act.symm
    · rw [ite_eq_left rfl]
  · constructor
    · rw [ite_eq_right hx, ite_eq_right hx]
    · rw [ite_eq_right hx, ite_eq_right hx]

theorem add_then_del_pointwise {V : Type} [DecidableEq V]
    (G : TimestampedGraph V) (e x : V × V) (h_rec : Nat)
    (h_abs : G.E e = false) (h_zero : G.H e = 0) :
    (T_del e (T_add e h_rec G)).1.E x = G.E x ∧
    (T_del e (T_add e h_rec G)).1.H x = G.H x := by
  dsimp [T_add, T_del]
  by_cases hx : x = e
  · subst hx
    constructor
    · rw [ite_eq_left rfl]
      exact h_abs.symm
    · rw [ite_eq_left rfl]
      exact h_zero.symm
  · constructor
    · rw [ite_eq_right hx, ite_eq_right hx]
    · rw [ite_eq_right hx, ite_eq_right hx]

#print axioms del_then_recorded_add_pointwise
#print axioms add_then_del_pointwise
```

**Verification Summary:**

The two closed terms certify the active-field identities. Deletion rollback reads the saved height, and addition rollback uses the absent-pair zero-height premise. The Lean kernel accepts both pointwise results.

### 1.5.Z Implications and Synthesis {#1.5.Z}

:::note[**Task Space**]
:::

Limiting dynamics to the bare minimum allows the system simply to make or break a link, as defined in the **Elementary Task Space** <Ref id="1.5.1" label="§1.5.1" />. The machinery of the universe is neutral, governed by the symmetric operators of **Edge Addition Task** <Ref id="1.5.2" label="§1.5.2" /> and **Edge Deletion Task** <Ref id="1.5.3" label="§1.5.3" />. This symmetry reveals itself as a vital feature of the theory because it ensures the universe is not structurally biased by its own mechanics toward either infinite density or total emptiness. The machinery of the universe is neutral. This allows the outcome to be determined by the interaction of the parts rather than the design of the tools. This neutrality is essential. If the laws of physics were biased toward creation, the universe would explode instantly. If they were biased toward destruction, it would vanish.

Structures can be built and dissolved with equal facility. This allows the system to explore its configuration space freely, ensuring the **Reversibility of Primitives** <Ref id="1.5.6" label="§1.5.6" />. This neutrality guarantees that any order that eventually emerges does so because of the thermodynamic rules of selection, not because the kinematic machinery was predisposed to produce it. By restricting the universe to these two operations, a conservation of possibility is established. Nothing is created that cannot be destroyed, and nothing is destroyed that cannot be recreated. This balance allows for a dynamic equilibrium to eventually form. It creates a state of flux that mimics the stability of matter.

This kinematic freedom is necessary but insufficient. While the ability to add and delete edges provides the vocabulary of change, it does not provide the rules of selection. A universe that can do anything at random will likely do nothing coherent. The verbs of the physical language, which are the creation and destruction of relations, are defined. However, the grammar that governs them remains to be formulated. We must assemble these primitives, definitions, and temporal constructs into a unified mathematical syntax. We turn to the formal synthesis of our ontological symbols.
