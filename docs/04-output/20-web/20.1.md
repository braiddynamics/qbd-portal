After reheating and nucleosynthesis, the emergent manifold faces a new crisis of structure: the hot plasma of photons and braid matter is a perfectly homogeneous, opaque scattering medium that cannot, on its own, organize into galaxies, clusters, or voids. The entry paradox is that thermodynamic equilibrium (the state the plasma achieves so efficiently) actively destroys the density contrasts needed for large-scale structure to grow. A universe in perfect thermal balance remains featureless forever.

Continuous fluid mechanics resolves this by appealing to quantum fluctuations in an externally specified inflaton field to seed the primordial overdensities, but this approach demands a background spacetime and arbitrary initial power spectra that are inputs rather than derivations. It cannot explain why the power spectrum is nearly scale-invariant, why baryon acoustic oscillations appear at a precisely computable scale, or why the CMB temperature anisotropies are suppressed to the level of $10^{-5}$ rather than a larger or smaller value.

Pre-geometric density correlations (planted by loop nucleation during inflation) act as potential wells that time-dilate photon motifs via the emergent Lapse function. Recombination severs the photon-braid coupling at a definite density threshold derivable from the Saha equation on the graph, releasing a fossilized blackbody spectrum whose acoustic peak positions and anisotropy amplitudes follow from the graph's causal geometry without free parameters.
